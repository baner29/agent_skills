# Banned AI Words and Patterns

This is a living watchlist used by the `orwell` skill. Users can edit this file manually at any time, and the agent can propose new entries when the user flags a term or edits it out repeatedly. Matching is case-insensitive and covers inflected forms (for example, *delve*, *delving*, *delved* count as one entry).

## 1. Dead AI Vocabulary

Avoid these words unless they are required as an exact, literal technical noun (for example, *"network topology"* or *"paradigm"* in philosophy of science).

- **Verbs:** accelerate, accentuate, align, bolster, captivate, delve, democratize, elevate, emphasize, empower, enhance, facilitate, foster, garner, harness, highlight, ignite, leverage, navigate (abstract), pioneer, redefine, reimagine, revolutionize, showcase, streamline, supercharge, surpass, synergize, transform, underscore, unleash, unlock, utilize.
- **Adjectives:** agile (overused), breathtaking, commendable, comprehensive, crucial, cutting-edge, disruptive, dynamic, enduring, game-changing, groundbreaking, holistic, immersive, indelible, innovative, intricate, leading-edge, meticulous, mission-critical, nuanced, paramount, pivotal, predictive, robust, seamless, state-of-the-art, transformative, turnkey, unparalleled, unprecedented, versatile, vibrant, visionary.
- **Nouns:** beacon, catalyst, cornerstone, ecosystem (abstract), game-changer, headway, interplay, landscape (abstract), paradigm, plethora, realm, synergy, tapestry, testament.
- **Adverbs and openers:** meticulously, seamlessly, undeniably, ultimately (as a filler opener), notably, importantly (as sentence openers).

## 2. Dead Transitions and Openers

Replace these mechanical phrases with direct statements or natural, context-specific links:

- "In today's [fast-paced / digital / modern] world..."
- "In the ever-evolving world of..."
- "It is important / worth noting that..."
- "In order to..." (use "to")
- "Let's dive in / explore / unpack..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "Moving forward..."
- "To put this in perspective..."
- "What makes this particularly interesting is..."
- "The implications here are..."
- "In other words..."
- "It goes without saying..."
- "That being said..." / "With that in mind..."
- "When it comes to..."
- "Furthermore..." / "Moreover..." / "Additionally..." (as habitual sentence openers; occasional use inside a sentence is fine)
- "In conclusion..." / "To summarize..." (when the paragraph already reads as a closing)

## 3. Structural and Rhetorical Clichés

- **Negative parallelisms:** "Not X, but Y", "It's not about X, it's about Y", "Not only X, but also Y", "Less X, more Y", "The question isn't X, it's Y".
- **Spotlight frames:** "Here is the part that matters", "The part most teams skip", "What really matters here is...", "The one thing to remember is...".
- **Engagement bait and hype:** "Let that sink in", "Read that again", "Full stop", "This changes everything", "Most people don't realize".
- **Indirect copula substitutes:** "serves as", "stands as", "marks a", "represents a", "boasts a", "features a" (prefer "is" or "has").
- **Trailing `-ing` participle commentary:** "...highlighting the importance of...", "...underscoring the need for...", "...paving the way for...".
- **False ranges and pairings:** "Whether you're a beginner or an expert...", "From startups to enterprises..." used as a sweep instead of a specific claim.
- **Rhetorical hooks:** opening questions the text immediately answers itself, and "Imagine a world where..." openers.
- **Vague attributions:** "Experts say...", "Studies show...", "Research suggests..." with no named source. Flag these for the user; never invent a source to fix them.
